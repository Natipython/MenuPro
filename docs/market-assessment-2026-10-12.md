# MenuMini / MenuPro: Addis Ababa market assessment

Research date: 12 October 2026. This is a commercial assessment and proposed experiment, not an approved price list or a change to product entitlements.

## Judgment

Free, MenuMini, and MenuPro can be a sensible progression within one product. The names and three-tier structure do not create a competitive advantage. Addis Ababa already has many publicly advertised offerings that substantially overlap the proposed product.

A basic QR menu faces strong price pressure. A dependable order and settlement workflow has more potential to earn recurring revenue, but it also creates much greater support and reliability obligations. Pro enters competition with restaurant POS, inventory, accounting, and fiscal systems. It cannot succeed merely by adding more themes or an AI button.

My recommendation is to continue toward a small paid pilot focused on independent cafes and casual restaurants in one reachable part of Addis. Build and measure the service that those particular venues need. Treat Pro as a later expansion driven by recurring operational requirements. There is not yet evidence of willingness to pay, retention, or profitable customer acquisition for our product.

## What this research establishes

Searches covered Ethiopian QR menus, table ordering, cafe software, restaurant POS, payment providers, and delivery platforms. I read public product and pricing pages, followed public demo links, and checked selected rendered pages and official corroborating sources. The inventory below includes small and early products as well as broader alternatives.

Evidence labels:

- **Public page:** Product or price text was retrievable from its public website during this research. This proves publication of an offer, not fulfillment of that offer.
- **Indexed page:** A search engine supplied text from the provider's own site, but live retrieval was incomplete or unavailable. Features and prices need current confirmation.
- **Public interface:** A customer-facing menu or app listing was accessible. This adds evidence of a distributed interface, not evidence of successful restaurant operations.
- **Corroborated announcement:** A named partner or regulator supplied supporting evidence for the specific launch or registration described.

No merchant account was created, no order or payment was submitted, and no provider or restaurant was contacted. I did not verify customer contracts, paid customer counts, payment integrations, offline behavior, financial results, or production uptime. Provider testimonials and counters remain self-reported. Retrieval failures can reflect tooling or access restrictions and are not evidence of a provider's service outage.

This is a substantial public-web competitor inventory, not a census of every business in Addis. Unindexed agencies, resellers, private POS installations, and WhatsApp-only services can be missing. Exact legal-company identity and common ownership between similarly positioned sites were not established; separate websites do not necessarily mean separate substantial businesses.

## Proposed tier boundaries

Use one product and shared data, with Free, Mini, and Pro plans. A venue should upgrade through the same account and retain its menu URL, QR artwork, and records. Separate commercial plans need not mean separate applications.

| Plan | Job it should perform | Sustainable boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Let a real venue publish and update a readable menu. | One venue, English/Amharic, basic presentation, prices/availability, shareable URL and printable QR. Generous text-menu capacity; measured image/storage limits; modest platform branding; self-service help. No ongoing staffed onboarding or live operational promises. |
| MenuMini | Help one venue receive and manage real orders and close its day. | Customer and staff-assisted orders in one queue, explicit acknowledgment, notes/modifiers, sold-out controls, cash/manual-transfer recording, authorized verification, basic daily close and exports. Assisted setup can carry a separate fee. |
| MenuPro | Coordinate a more complicated restaurant operation. | Station routing, table bills with additions/merge/split, stronger roles, shift reconciliation, branches, proven printing and payment/POS integration. Ingredient inventory should be offered only once recipes, purchases, wastage, portions, and costs are maintained reliably. |

Guests should pay nothing to browse or place ordinary venue orders across all plans. Readability, accessible language, basic security, correct prices, and data export should not be premium features. Do not insert rival restaurant advertisements into a venue's menu, cap ordinary scans mid-service, or require a guest account for routine dine-in use.

Free can lower acquisition friction, but it can attract venues that will never upgrade. An owner who only needs a menu may remain satisfied permanently. That is acceptable only if Free is inexpensive to operate and does not require continuing personal service. Unlimited free photography, translation, AI calls, menu entry, visits, or support would create a cost obligation without revenue.

Paid plans must sell a result a particular owner values. More themes, QR generation, language toggles, and AI import have limited defensibility because multiple alternatives already advertise them. Assisted migration, quick training, reliable floor behavior, and truthful reconciliation can be more valuable, but competitors can offer those too. We must demonstrate an advantage rather than claim it from this research.

## Pricing and economics

The advertised spread includes basic annual menus below 3,000 ETB and operational systems at several thousand ETB per month. These are comparison points, not proof of market-clearing prices, paid conversion, or sustainable margins. Annual prepayment and monthly subscriptions impose different cash-flow demands on the owner; packages with setup, stands, hardware, and tax are not directly comparable.

I would test a Mini offer around **500–1,000 ETB/venue/month** only after it delivers a useful order-and-close workflow. A **2,000–5,000 ETB/month** Pro experiment would require a demonstrable operational benefit. These are hypotheses for interviewing and charging pilot customers, not recommended final prices. A costly support model can make even these prices unviable. A menu-only offer should be evaluated against the cheaper annual competitors instead.

Price assisted setup separately when it includes data entry, travel, printing, and training. Offer a trial without confusing it with a permanent free tier. List payment-provider fees separately from the software subscription, and confirm applicable ETB merchant terms. The current [Chapa pricing page](https://chapa.co/pricing) has country-dependent presentation; its default Canadian fee is not an established Ethiopian fee for our product.

Illustrative arithmetic, with no claim that these prices, workloads, or conversion rates are observed:

- **100 paid venues × 750 ETB/month = 75,000 ETB/month gross subscription revenue.**
- **1,000 paid venues × 750 = 750,000 ETB/month gross.**
- **20 minutes of support per venue/month** would mean approximately **33 hours** for 100 venues or **333 hours** for 1,000, before setup, sales, travel, billing, incident response, and engineering.
- If **1,000 free venues yield 5% paid conversion**, that would be **50 paying venues**, or **37,500 ETB/month** at this hypothetical price. Five percent is a stress-test assumption, not a forecast.

Revenue still has to fund staff/founder time, hosting and backups, image delivery, notifications, payment/provider charges where applicable, travel, acquisition, printing, tax, bad debt, and refunds. Free users consume some of those resources too. Birr revenue and foreign-currency infrastructure bills can move differently.

Measure monthly contribution per paid venue after recurring direct costs, acquisition/onboarding cost, time to recover that cost, renewal, and actual staff usage. Subscription receipts, menu scans, restaurant sales handled, and our profit are different quantities. Do not mistake order value passing directly to restaurants for MenuMini revenue.

## Owner, staff, and guest perspectives

**Owners:** A cheap subscription can still be poor value if staff must enter each order again in a separate till or fiscal system. The existing register, accounting procedure, and payment account do not disappear when a QR is installed. Identify whether a venue wants menu publishing, service improvement, cash control, or a complete POS replacement. Selling all four at once increases risk and implementation cost.

**Staff:** Successful ordering needs someone responsible for the queue during every shift, a suitable device, clear acknowledgment, and a workable kitchen handoff. Staff should be able to enter an order for a guest, correct a mistake through an auditable action, and get help without becoming unpaid technical support. Reducing handwriting can help; extra screens, repeated alerts, surveillance fears, or loss of tips can produce resistance. Interview staff as well as the owner.

**Guests:** Useful benefits are clear current prices, readable language, accurate availability, understanding portions/modifiers, and quicker service or checkout. QR-only service can exclude guests without a suitable phone, charge, data, or comfort using it. Keep paper and waiter assistance available. Avoid large image downloads, forced accounts, unexpected tips/charges, and automatic marketing enrollment. A guest who submitted an order must know whether the venue actually accepted it.

**Local context:** Ethio telecom's [2025/26 report](https://www.ethiotelecom.et/2025-26-budget-year-annual-business-performance-report/), dated 23 July 2026, reports smartphone penetration of **45.2%**, **60.6 million** telebirr customers onboarded, and **19.48 million 90-day active users**. These are provider-reported national indicators, not Addis diner percentages or counts of unique prospective menu users. They support both opportunity and caution: digital payment familiarity exists, but registration, smartphone access, and routine restaurant use are different things.

Shared platters, multiple people adding to one table, extra rounds of drinks, changing service staff, cash plus transfer, bill splits, and refunds need explicit treatment. A cart submitted by one phone is not automatically the table's final bill. Hotels also introduce room billing and existing property-management systems. These practical differences justify keeping the initial customer segment narrow.

AI menu import can reduce typing, but owners must check prices, portions, fasting descriptions, translations, and ingredient/allergen information before publication. Do not invent dietary safety claims from a photograph. Sales reporting should not be labeled profit reporting unless the necessary cost records are actually present.

## What popularity would change

Popularity is conditional; there is no evidence that it will occur or a defensible probability for it. These are plausible consequences if usage expands:

| Situation | Likely business pressure | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| Many signups, few active or paying venues | Hosting and support growth without a healthy business. Public counters can conceal this. | Track live published menus, routine staff use, conversion, contribution, and renewals by cohort. Limit expensive free services. |
| More paying venues in one neighborhood | Referrals, easier travel, stronger references, and repeated learning about similar workflows. | Standardize setup/training, collect measured results with consent, and build local support capacity. |
| Expansion to hundreds of operating venues | Peak-hour incidents, menu changes, staff turnover, late subscription payments, and device/printing questions accumulate. | Document escalation and support hours, train venue champions, maintain incident procedures, and budget operations staff. |
| Competitors notice traction | Price discounts, cloned features, bundles, poached prospects, and demands for existing POS integration may increase. | Differentiate through delivered reliability and service; offer clear migration/export. Explore partnerships without depending on one channel. |
| One payment or privacy failure affects many venues | Duplicate claims, account changes, fake screenshots, unauthorized adjustments, or exposed guest data can damage both our brand and restaurants. | Keep restaurant money going to verified merchant accounts, use appropriate payment partners, verify settlement, protect sensitive records, and provide traceable corrections/refunds. |
| Product becomes essential to daily service | A menu outage can interrupt ordering and damage customer trust; a wide outage has shared consequences. | Test backup restoration and failure handling, preserve clear pending/accepted distinctions, and rehearse a paper/staff fallback and reconciliation. |
| Demand moves toward Pro | Branch reports, kitchen hardware, inventory, table bills, accounting, and fiscal integration turn into commercial expectations. | Accept only supported requirements, prove integrations, and price implementation/support honestly. Avoid custom work that destroys the standard product. |

Manual payment verification remains the venue staff's task in the selected first version. MenuMini records an authorized person's verification; it does not magically verify a bank transfer. A screenshot, AI assessment, or customer's assertion is not proof that the intended account received the correct funds. During growth, manual verification can become a cashier bottleneck, making dependable provider confirmation a potential future upgrade.

Payment-system operation and fiscal compliance are substantive capabilities. The [NBE register](https://nbe.gov.et/payment-instrument-issuers-system-operators/) identifies existing authorized payment operators; it does not determine the legal classification of every possible future MenuMini feature. Confirm requirements before offering custody, settlement, or regulated payment services. Likewise, an order summary or payment acknowledgment should not be marketed as an approved fiscal invoice without establishing the applicable approval/integration. This research is not a legal compliance audit.

There is no automatic strong network effect from a diner scanning the product at many unrelated restaurants. Each venue can use a different menu provider. Growth can improve brand recognition, referrals, and operating knowledge; a consumer discovery marketplace would introduce a separate business with additional acquisition and trust obligations.

## A realistic decision experiment

1. **Talk to 10–15 owner/manager decision makers and observe five actual service periods.** Ask about their last costly error, present POS/payment workflow, who would monitor a queue, recent menu-printing cost, connectivity/device constraints, and what would make them cancel. Include venues content with paper and those already using competitors. Inspect the existing workflow before pitching a replacement.
2. **Obtain comparison evidence.** With appropriate authorization for any outreach, request current written competitor quotes/demos and speak to restaurant references. Compare subscription, setup, tax, stands, devices, support hours, offline claims, fiscal compatibility, export, and cancellation. Our public research is not a substitute for these checks.
3. **Run a 4–6 week pilot at three to five independent venues in one area.** Complete actual merchant login and menu publication, persistent orders, staff handoff, manual verification, day-close records, and practical failure handling before depending on it during service. At least some owners should pay an agreed price; a free friendly trial does not prove recurring demand.
4. **Record before/after outcomes.** Measure order acknowledgment time, missing/duplicate orders, corrections, cashier reconciliation time and discrepancies, diner completion and requests for help, staff usage, support time, total onboarding cost, and paid renewal. Do not infer a revenue uplift from scan counts alone.
5. **Use renewal and workload as the decision.** Three independently acquired paying venues renewing at the intended price, with steady use and manageable support, would be an encouraging next-step gate, not proof of citywide product-market fit. Unresolved lost orders, incorrect paid balances, or continual founder intervention should stop expansion until repaired. If owners value only a menu, simplify the product and economics. If table/shift/branch problems repeatedly drive willingness to pay, those observations should shape Pro.

## Implication for the present workspace

The earlier audit found working demonstrations, and the subsequent work created a validated database/contract foundation. That foundation does not establish a deployed end-to-end commercial product. Real merchant accounts, shared menu publication, staff operation, and the complete live order/payment flow still need implementation and field verification.

This assessment supports a narrow build followed by a paid pilot. It does not support announcing a fully operational Free/Mini/Pro platform, promising automatic payment verification, assuming a new empty market, or treating competitor marketing counts as verified demand.
